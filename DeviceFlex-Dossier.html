01

Executive summary & product overview

Device protection is the only product a customer pays for monthly and hopes never to use. DeviceFlex turns it into a membership that does something every month — and rebuilds the claim itself around evidence only a carrier can see.

The vision

DeviceFlex is AT&T Protect Advantage reimagined as an AI-powered device membership. Not a competing product — a layer on top of the $5.2B protection programme AT&T already operates and the claim-to-upgrade experience already on its roadmap. The ultimate goal is to convert a passive insurance line into an engagement surface: something a member opens, uses and benefits from between claims.

Underneath the membership sits the part that is genuinely novel and genuinely defensible: a decision architecture in which probabilistic models are confined to perception and every consequential decision is made by a pure, replayable function over account state. That split is what makes the system trustworthy, auditable, and — critically for an IP filing — a technical mechanism rather than an abstract idea.

Target audience

Primary · the covered household

21.4M AT&T mobility customers, ~18M of them on a protection feature. The member who cracks a screen, loses a phone, or simply wants to know what they're paying for.

Secondary · retail & care

Store associates and ProTech agents, who inherit a claim already verified, already priced, and already routed to the right node with stock on hand.

Tertiary · the underwriter

Asurion and its underwriter, who receive a complete, corroborated, auditable claim payload instead of an affidavit and a phone call.

Core value proposition

For the member: you find out what a claim costs and how it resolves before you commit to anything, and the network helps prove your story so you don't have to. For AT&T: a mechanism that closes the 18%-online-versus-70%-retail attach gap while making automated underwriting decisions reproducible enough to defend.

Problem statement & market context

Device protection is a mature, commoditised category. Verizon, T-Mobile and AT&T all resell substantially the same Asurion-administered product with substantially the same terms. Differentiation on price is impossible; differentiation on experience has not been seriously attempted.

Where it breaks today

The deductible is a surprise

A member discovers their $275 replacement deductible at the counter or at step two of a claim form. The single most important number in the product is the one hidden longest.

Loss claims run on trust alone

An administrator that does not operate a network cannot check whether a phone really went dark. It can believe the customer or demand an affidavit. This is where most claim fraud lives, and honest customers pay for it in premiums and friction.

Coverage is bound to a handset

The policy names an IMEI; the backup belongs to whichever cloud account the customer set up months earlier. Restoring takes days and only works if they did something right long before the incident.

Zero utility between claims

The product activates only during a disaster. There is nothing to open, nothing to check, and no reason to value it in the eleven months nothing happens.

Before anything breaks

They know their deductible, their device's health, and that a spare has been moved to a store near them if one is trending toward failure.

Unique differentiators & competitive edge

One sentence carries the whole moat: AT&T runs the network. Asurion, Apple and the insurers do not.

The unfair advantage

For every line on the account, AT&T already knows when the device last reached a tower, which one, whether it went dark abruptly or wound down cleanly, and whether the SIM has since turned up in a different handset. A protection administrator without a network can ask a customer to fill in a form. It has no equivalent, and no path to one — this is a structural asymmetry, not a head start.

05

Patentability & intellectual property

The governing constraint

Under the Alice/Mayo two-step test, “a tiered device-protection membership with better ARPU” is not patentable. Insurance and pricing tiers are a fundamental economic practice — an abstract idea — and implementing one “on an app” fails step one.

To survive, a claim must clear step two: an inventive concept delivering a concrete technical improvement to how the machine works, not what the business achieves. Courts uphold Enfish (a specific data structure that improved computer performance) and McRO (a specific rules-based technique); they reject “AI-powered system” claims describing only what software does.

We patent the mechanism, not the membership.

Novelty — the five mechanisms

1 · Network-Signal-Corroborated Claim Engine

Fuses carrier-side device-presence telemetry — last-seen-on-network, disconnection pattern, SIM/IMEI status, cell-site context — with the claim flow to auto-corroborate loss and theft and gate fraud before a human reviews it.

Technical effect: improves the machine's fraud-decision accuracy using signals structurally unavailable to Asurion and Apple. Not a business rule — a machine that ingests network signals and produces a faster, more accurate decision.

2 · Line-Anchored Restoration Manifest

A continuously reconciled, device-agnostic state manifest keyed to the subscriber identifier rather than the IMEI, with handsets as pointers, plus a cryptographic continuity token binding coverage and manifest pointer to the line.

Technical effect: reduces restore from days to under two minutes and makes the 15-minute swap a provisioning improvement rather than a logistics promise. This is the Enfish argument — a data structure that makes the system work better.

3 · Deterministic, Replayable Decision Ledger

Every automated decision is emitted by a pure function and recorded with digests of its canonical input and output. Replay re-executes the same function against the recorded input and compares digests.

Technical effect: eliminates model drift and enables verifiable, reproducible automated underwriting — a specific improvement over black-box ML, and the answer to “how do we know the AI was right?”

4 · Multi-Path Resolution Optimizer with Inventory Pre-Staging

A deterministic device-health decay index that, on crossing a threshold, pre-positions a matching replacement plus restore snapshot at the nearest capable fulfilment node.

Technical effect: ties software decisions to physical supply-chain movement — a concrete real-world effect, which is precisely what clears Alice step two.

5 · Device-Attested Underwriting Gate

On-device diagnostics produce a signed condition attestation over sensors, battery, housing and identity; enrolment is gated on a valid, recent, signature-verified attestation.

Technical effect: prevents adverse selection and pre-existing-damage fraud through a technical security mechanism rather than a policy rule, aligned with device-identity standards work.

The organising principle

Every “AI” decision is two layers: an AI perception layer that reads photos, network signals and questions, and a deterministic decision layer that turns those facts into an auditable verdict.

The AI makes it smart. The determinism makes it trustworthy and patentable. No competitor can copy the perception layer, because it runs on AT&T's own network signals.

Non-obviousness — why a PHOSITA would not expect this

The prevailing direction of travel in automated underwriting is more model, not less: end-to-end ML scoring the whole claim. A person having ordinary skill in the art, handed a vision model and a claim flow, would wire the model's confidence directly into the approve/deny path. This design does the opposite — it deliberately quarantines the probabilistic component to perception and forbids it from touching the decision, then makes that boundary auditable by hashing both sides.

The second non-obvious step is inverting the key of the restoration data structure. Every incumbent binds coverage and backup to the handset, because the handset is what breaks and what gets replaced. Keying to the subscriber line — the one identifier the carrier controls and which survives the incident — is counter-intuitive precisely because the line is not the thing being insured.

Illustrative claim framework

For the IDF; not final legal language.

Independent claim 1 · system

A device-protection system comprising: a subscriber-identifier-anchored state manifest continuously reconciled from a covered device; a network-telemetry ingestion module that corroborates a claim event against carrier-side device-presence signals; a deterministic decision engine that emits a replayable decision trace ranking resolution paths against live fulfilment-node inventory and technician availability; and a provisioning module that restores the manifest to a replacement device bound to the same subscriber identifier.

Claim 2 · dependent

The system of claim 1, wherein the network telemetry comprises last-seen-on-network time, disconnection pattern, and cell-site location, and wherein the decision engine withholds a device-blocklist submission when the telemetry contradicts the reported claim event.

The withholding limitation is doing real work here — it distinguishes the claim from a system that merely displays telemetry alongside a decision.

Claim 3 · dependent

The system of claim 1, wherein the decision engine computes a device-health decay index from battery capacity, impact-risk profile, warranty state and backup currency, and pre-positions a replacement device and restore snapshot at a fulfilment node when the index crosses a deterministic threshold.

Claim 4 · dependent

The system of claim 1, wherein enrolment is gated by a signed on-device condition attestation, the attestation comprising an enumerated check set, a derived health score and a signature over a canonical serialisation thereof, and wherein the gate is enforced in the state-mutation layer such that no presentation-layer path can bypass it.

Claim 5 · dependent

The system of claim 1, wherein each decision trace comprises a digest over a canonical, key-ordered serialisation of the decision function's input and output, and wherein replaying a trace re-executes the recorded function against the recorded input and compares the resulting digest to the recorded digest.

Canonical key ordering is not a detail — without it two structurally identical inputs digest differently and the ledger proves nothing.

Prior-art framing

Existing solutions rely on manual claim forms, human fraud review, handset-bound coverage, and shipping-based replacement with customer-managed backup. None corroborate claims using carrier network telemetry; none anchor a restoration manifest to the subscriber line; none use a deterministic, replayable decision ledger. This invention uniquely integrates network-rooted verification, line-anchored provisioning and reproducible decisioning into one system.

Edge cases, handled explicitly

Network contradicts the member

The claim proceeds to a specialist rather than auto-approving, and the blocklist submission is withheld. Blocking on disputed evidence would strand a working device.

Vision provider unreachable

Falls back to the deterministic on-device assessment and says so in the UI. A demo must never show a spinner that never resolves.

Device already damaged at enrolment

The attestation fails, enrolment is refused, and the refusal is evidenced — the failing check set is stored, not just asserted.

Claim filed outside the 60-day window

Still accepted, flagged for Asurion review rather than silently rejected, with the day count stated.

No incident time given

Corroboration degrades gracefully to a pattern-only verdict at reduced confidence rather than fabricating agreement.

Malfunction or battery claim

The engine declines to speak: a device that will not charge can still register normally, so network presence is neither support nor contradiction. Diagnostics carry it.

Stale persisted account state

A record saved before a schema field existed still loads; reconcile() backfills manifests, ledger and attestations on read.

Tier downgrade drops devices

The plan-change flow names which devices lose coverage before the change is committed, rather than silently truncating the pool.

Data flow, end to end

  1. Ingress. Photos are read client-side, converted to data URIs, and posted to a TanStack server function. They never touch a third-party host from the browser.
  2. Perception. The server function lazily imports vision.server.ts so the provider module never enters the client bundle. The API key is read from process.env server-side only.
  3. Validation. The model's reply is parsed out of any markdown fence and validated against a flat Zod schema. Failure throws a VisionError, and the caller falls back to the deterministic assessment.
  4. Decision. Structured facts plus account state enter pure functions. now is passed as a parameter rather than read from the clock, because a function that calls Date.now() internally is not replayable.
  5. Recording. decide() is a combinator, not a logging wrapper — it runs the function and returns { value, trace }, so a recorded decision and an executed one are the same event by construction.
  6. Reconciliation. Every state mutation passes through reconcile(), which recomputes the score and rebuilds every line manifest. “Continuously reconciled” is therefore true by construction, not by discipline.
  7. Egress. Whatever the claim type, one payload shape reaches Asurion. Swapping the simulated transport for a real POST changes nothing upstream.

Production shape — what would change

The prototype's persistence is deliberately trivial. A deployed system keeps the same decision architecture and replaces the edges:

State
Postgres for account and claim state; the manifest as a versioned JSONB document keyed by subscriber identifier, with row-level security by line.
Ledger
Append-only, write-once storage with an S3 Object Lock or equivalent retention policy — a ledger that can be edited proves nothing.
Telemetry
Read through AT&T's internal network-data APIs rather than a seeded file, with strict purpose limitation and a short retention window.
Signing
Attestations signed in the device's secure enclave and verified against the manufacturer root; continuity tokens signed by a carrier key in an HSM.
Inference
Vision serving behind an internal gateway with per-tenant quotas, request signing and a circuit breaker to the deterministic path.
Scale
~0.9% monthly claim rate on 18M subscribers is ~162k claims/month — trivial write volume. The load is read-heavy dashboard traffic, which is edge-cacheable per line, and burst inference at incident peaks, which queues behind the deterministic fallback.
08

Security, compliance & governance

Stated plainly

This is a prototype. Its “signatures” are FNV-1a digests over canonical JSON, not signed credentials — a browser has no key material and no secure element, and faking that convincingly would be worse than saying so. The structure is what is claimable; the cryptography is what a deployment supplies. Every such boundary is labelled in the source.

Data protection, as designed for production

In transit

TLS 1.3 throughout. Vision requests leave only from the server runtime, never the browser, so no provider credential is ever client-reachable.

At rest

AES-256 on the vault and claim store. Photographic evidence is the most sensitive artefact in the system and carries the shortest retention — deleted once the claim closes.

Authentication

Federated through the existing AT&T ID, not a parallel identity. The prototype's demo login is explicitly not authentication and is labelled as such in code.

Authorization

Line-scoped. The manifest is keyed by subscriber identifier, which makes row-level security natural: a member can only ever read manifests for lines on their account.

The irreversible action

Blocklist submission requires explicit informed consent, is refused when telemetry disputes the report, and is enforced in the mutation layer rather than the UI.

Decision auditability

Every automated underwriting decision carries a replayable trace. This is a compliance asset, not just a patent claim — it is what an adverse-action review needs.

Regulatory alignment

AI governance

The perception/decision split is itself the governance mechanism. A model cannot approve, deny, price or blocklist — it can only describe what it observed. That constraint is architectural rather than procedural, which means it holds under pressure and can be demonstrated rather than attested.

Cost to serve

The economics are unusually favourable because the expensive component is bounded by the claim rate, not by traffic. At ~0.9% monthly claims, roughly 99% of member sessions invoke no inference at all — dashboards, scores, vault views and eligibility checks are all pure functions over state the system already holds.

Inference

Three images per damage claim, once. A small VLM on batched serving is cents per claim, and the deterministic fallback caps the downside at zero.

Storage

The vault dominates. Per-device allocation rather than flat tier ceilings means a three-device family is not billed capacity for five.

Fulfilment

The real cost. Pre-staging shifts spend from expedited shipping to inventory positioning — cheaper per event, and it converts a cost into a differentiator.

How pricing scales with the customer

Expansion is device-count-driven rather than seat-driven, which suits a household: a member who adds a tablet, a watch or a child's phone moves up the ladder without a renegotiation. The accessory perk is a deliberate margin sacrifice that buys a monthly reason to open the app — engagement that a protection product otherwise has no way to purchase.

Reverse-engineering defence & trade secrets

A published patent teaches the mechanism. That is the trade: disclosure for exclusivity. What it does not teach is the tuning — and the tuning is where a competitor would have to spend years even with the patent in hand.

Server-side barriers

Never in the client bundle

The vision provider module is lazily imported inside a server function, so it does not enter the client graph. Prompt text, schema and provider identity stay server-side.

Telemetry stays behind the API

The browser receives a verdict and its reasons, never the raw device-presence record. A scraped client learns the shape of the answer, not the signal.

Thresholds served, not shipped

In production the posture thresholds, decay-index weights and tier boundaries are configuration fetched per session, not constants compiled into JavaScript.

Ledger is append-only server-side

Traces are written where the client cannot reach them. Replay is offered as an API operation, not as client-side re-execution.

Technical risk & system degradation

The design principle for every failure mode: degrade to deterministic. Every dependency in this system has a defined answer for what happens when it is unavailable, and in every case the answer is a working, honest, lower-fidelity product — never a spinner.

Vendor lock-in

Deliberately minimised at the two places it would hurt. The vision bridge speaks the OpenAI-compatible chat-completions shape, which Fireworks, vLLM and an AMD cloud-native endpoint all implement — switching provider is three environment variables. The server build targets Nitro presets, so Vercel, Cloudflare and Node are configuration rather than migration. The genuine lock-in is to AT&T's own network data, which is the point.

Honest risks

CPNI approval is the long pole

Using cell-site data for claim adjudication is a privacy and regulatory question before it is an engineering one. Shadow mode in Phase 2 exists to build that case with evidence.

Corroboration can be wrong

A phone left in a drawer looks like a lost phone. This is why the mechanism flags rather than refuses, and why a contradicted claim withholds the blocklist instead of denying the claim.

Pre-staging carries inventory cost

Positioning stock against a prediction ties up capital. The threshold is the control, and it should start conservative and loosen on measured hit rate.

Cold start & the data flywheel

Why there is no cold start problem

This is the quiet advantage of building inside an incumbent. On day one the system already knows every device on the account, its purchase date, warranty state, installment position, claim history and — uniquely — its live network presence. There is no empty state to design around, because AT&T has been collecting the inputs for years for other reasons.

Concretely: a brand-new member with zero interaction history still gets a populated Protection Score, a real deductible for every device, a genuine eligibility verdict against both enrolment doors, and corroboration available from their first claim. The deterministic engine needs state, not history — which is precisely why the decision layer being deterministic matters commercially and not only legally.

The flywheel

01
Members file claims

Each one pairs a reported account of events with the network's record of the same window.

02
Outcomes label the data

Whether a specialist upheld or overturned a corroboration verdict is a free, high-quality label — generated by a process that has to happen anyway.

03
The anomaly model sharpens

Disconnect-pattern classification improves against real adjudicated outcomes rather than synthetic assumptions.

04
Thresholds tighten deterministically

Better perception raises confidence, which lets the deterministic thresholds narrow — more claims auto-approve, fewer honest members wait.

05
Failures teach pre-staging

Every actual device failure is a label for the decay index. Hit rate improves, inventory positioning gets cheaper, same-day swap rate climbs.

06
The ledger makes it safe to iterate

Because every historical decision can be replayed, a threshold change can be evaluated against the entire back catalogue before it ships. This is the flywheel's bearing — it is what lets the loop turn quickly without turning recklessly.
